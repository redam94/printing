It happens, usually because a measurement was off or something on the real object was not where it seemed. Fixing it is part of the service.

## Raise a *Reprint or fix an earlier part* request

Include:

- **The original request key** (for example `PRINT-12`), if you have it.
- **What is wrong**, as precisely as you can: *the slot is about 1 mm too narrow*, *the clip snapped at the corner*, *it fits but rocks*.
- **A photo of the part in place**, ideally with a ruler or the object it should fit.
- **Whether you want it changed or just printed again** — sometimes a print is simply flawed.

## What it costs

| Situation | Cost |
|---|---|
| The print failed, or the part came out flawed | Free reprint |
| The part does not match the design or the quote | Free reprint |
| The design was built on a measurement that turned out wrong | Materials and print time only, no new design fee |
| You want it different from what you confirmed | Quoted as a change: the print price, plus the {price.design_adapt} fitting fee if the design has to change |

## Making the second one right

A part that "almost fits" is the most useful thing you can give us — it turns a guess into a measurement:

- Say **which way** it is wrong and **by how much**, even roughly: *2 mm too deep*, *a hair too tight*.
- If it rocks, say **which corner** lifts.
- If it snapped, say **when**: while fitting it, or after a month of use. The first is a fit problem, the second a material or thickness problem.

::: tip Keep the first one
Don't throw away a part that doesn't fit until the replacement is in your hands. It is the reference for what changed, and sometimes a 30-second trim with a craft knife is all it needs.
:::
