Good measurements are the difference between a part that fits first time and a second round. Everything in **millimetres**, please.

## The basics

- **Use calipers** if you have them; a steel ruler otherwise. A tape measure is fine for anything over 100 mm.
- **Measure what the part touches:** the object it holds, the gap it sits in, the edge it clips onto, hole spacing (centre to centre) and hole diameters.
- **Measure twice**, and write down which number you are least sure of.
- **Mark guesses with a tilde** (`~40`) so the design leaves room there.
- **Photograph it with a ruler** beside it, square-on, and attach the photo.

## What to measure, by kind of part

| If the part… | Measure |
|---|---|
| Holds an object | The object's width, depth and height at its widest, and where it tapers |
| Clips over an edge | The thickness of the edge, how far it overhangs, and what is underneath |
| Sits in a gap | The gap at its narrowest, and the depth available |
| Mounts on screws | Hole centre-to-centre spacing, hole diameter, and the screw head size |
| Replaces a broken part | Every dimension of the original, plus photos of both sides. Send the broken part if you can |
| Fits a cable or pipe | The outside diameter, and whether the plug is bigger than the cable |

## A worked example

A phone stand for a desk. Useful: phone 147 × 71 × 8 mm **with the case on**, charging socket 9 mm wide and centred on the bottom edge, desk depth 300 mm, and the phone should sit at about 60° for video calls.

Not useful: "it's for a phone".

## Where the tolerance goes

Printed parts are accurate to roughly ±0.2 mm, and clearance is added on purpose so things go together:

- **Slides in and out easily:** 0.3 mm of gap per side.
- **Press fit, stays put:** about 0.1 mm of interference.
- **Rotates freely:** 0.4 mm per side.

You do not need to add that yourself — give the true measurement and say how tight it should feel.

::: tip When you can't measure it
Send photos with any everyday object in shot for scale and say what it is: a coin, a credit card, a standard pencil. A rough design and a test print is a normal way to start when the original is gone.
:::
