## Heat is the thing to watch

| Material | Comfortable up to | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|
| PLA | ~50 °C | A car in summer, a sunny windowsill, above a radiator |
| PETG | ~70 °C | Boiling water, an oven, a dishwasher's drying cycle |
| TPU | ~75 °C | Sustained heat under load |
| ASA | ~90 °C | Direct flame, engine bays |

A part that softens does not melt into a puddle: it sags slowly under its own weight or its load. If yours has drooped, it is in a hotter place than its material allows, and reprinting it in PETG or ASA fixes it for good.

## Sunlight and weather

PLA and PETG go brittle after months of direct UV. ASA is made for it. Outdoors, expect a PLA part to last a season and an ASA part to last years.

## Cleaning

- Hand-wash in **cool** water with mild soap; no dishwasher.
- No solvents, no bleach, no alcohol on painted parts.
- A soft brush gets dust out of layer lines and textures.

## Gluing and repairing

- **PLA and PETG:** cyanoacrylate (superglue) gives a strong joint on a clean, scuffed surface. Two-part epoxy is stronger still for a load-bearing repair.
- **TPU:** use a flexible adhesive; superglue cracks off.
- **A snapped part** can often be glued as a stopgap, but a break usually means the part wants a thicker section or a different orientation. Send a photo of the break and it can be redesigned.

## Making it last

- Keep a load along the part, not pulling the layers apart.
- Don't over-tighten screws into plastic; brass inserts are used where a screw comes in and out often.
- Store spares out of sunlight.

::: tip Printed parts are cheap to replace
Most of these parts cost a few pounds of plastic and an hour or two of machine time. If one wears out, the design is on file and a repeat print costs the material and the printing, not the design.
:::
